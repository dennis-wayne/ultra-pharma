A review timeline depends on more than the authority's assessment. The file you submit and the way you answer queries also matter.

## Where the time goes

A review can include assessment periods and pauses while the applicant responds to questions. An incomplete response can lead to another query or another turn in the queue.

That is why a series of small gaps can add up to a longer review. Fixing the source of a query is often more useful than replying quickly.

## What you can prepare

### Check the right requirements

Confirm how the product is classified before you build the file. Classification affects which requirements apply. A gap review before submission helps your team address questions about the requirements while there is still time to change the dossier.

### Make the dossier consistent

The quality and clinical sections need to describe the same product. Cross-check the sections before filing so an assessor does not have to ask your team to reconcile them.

### Answer the question asked

Reply to each query with the relevant evidence and a clear reference to the file. If the question needs clarification, address that directly. Reframing it without resolving the issue can leave it open.

## What this means for your filing plan

Allow time for review and query responses as well as the submission itself. A pre-submission audit can help you identify gaps before the authority does. You control the preparation and the quality of your responses; the authority controls its review and decision.
